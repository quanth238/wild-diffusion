\section{Introduction}
Score-based diffusion models are highly effective generative models, but under limited data they may fit an overly narrow support of the training distribution, leading to overfitting and weak local generalization \citep{song2021sde,karras2022edm}. A propose idea is to train the model not only on the standard forward corruption process, but also on \emph{difficult nearby perturbations} of that process.

Instead of adversarially perturbing an image once in pixel space, we study adversarial perturbations of the \emph{forward corruption trajectory}. We view the perturbed trajectory as a family of hard training views generated from a path-space ambiguity set around the
standard forward process. This places the proposal closer to robust training over stochastic-process couplings than to a direct replacement of the diffusion model itself. Our connection to robust learning is therefore the following:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item start from a clean image $x_0 \sim \data$;
    \item run the usual forward corruption process;
    \item allow an adapted perturbation of the trajectory inside a constrained
    path-space ambiguity set;
    \item train the denoiser on these harder but still local corrupted views;
    \item test whether this improves limited-data robustness without degrading
    clean generative fidelity too much.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Main design principle.}
The perturbation should be constrained at the \emph{coupling level}. Concretely, we will work with a synchronous coupling between a reference forward path and a controlled forward path, and we restrict the feasible perturbations by a hard per-step constraint on the conditional innovation kernels. This is motivated by causal/bicausal transport and adapted Wasserstein process distances, which constrain couplings in a filtration-aware way rather than comparing paths only after marginalizing out time structure
\citep{backhoff2017causal,backhoff2021process,gunasingam2025adapted,szolgyenyi2025adapted}.

\section{Problem setup}
Let $\X = \R^d$ denote the data space and let $x_0 \sim \data$ be a training sample. We begin with a standard forward diffusion SDE on the time interval $[0,T]$:
\begin{equation}
    dX_t = f(X_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dW_t,
    \qquad X_0 = x_0,
    \label{eq:base_forward}
\end{equation}
where $W_t$ is a standard Wiener process, $f:\X\times[0,T]\to\X$ is the drift, and $g:[0,T]\to\R_+$ is the diffusion scale. This process defines the usual family of noisy marginals $p_t$ used in score-based generative modeling \citep{song2021sde}.

\subsection{Standard and perturbed forward paths}
Instead of introducing an arbitrary adversarial path law, we work with a \emph{coupled} pair of forward processes: a standard path \(X_t\), which follows the reference diffusion, and a perturbed path \(\tilde X_t\), which is driven by the same stochastic noise but is nudged by an adapted perturbation process \(A_t\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    dX_t &= f(X_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dW_t, \\
    d\tilde X_t &= f(\tilde X_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dW_t + dA_t.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coupled_forward}
\end{equation}
Here, the two paths share the same Brownian motion \(W_t\) and differ only through the perturbation \(A_t\). This shared-noise construction forms a synchronous coupling, which isolates the effect of the perturbation while keeping the perturbed path close to the standard forward process. Such a coupling is natural for adapted transport and process-space comparison. \citep{szolgyenyi2025adapted,gunasingam2025adapted}.

\subsection{Bicausal path-space ambiguity set}
% The central object is not a soft energy penalty but a feasible set of adapted couplings between the reference path law and the perturbed path law.

At a high level, we want the controlled process to remain inside a small bicausal ambiguity set around the reference process. This is consistent with the nested-distance / bicausal Wasserstein viewpoint, where couplings between stochastic processes must respect temporal information and filtration \citep{backhoff2017causal,backhoff2021process}.

For implementation, we will use a discrete-time approximation and impose the constraint directly on the one-step conditional kernels. This gives a concrete and computationally tractable surrogate for a local bicausal ball.

\section{Training Objective}
Let $p_t^u$ denote the distribution of
the perturbed forward process sample $X_t^u$ at time $t$. The target score field is:
\begin{equation}
    s_\theta(x,t) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t^u(x).
\end{equation}

Our goal is therefore to learn a score model that remains accurate even under
adversarially perturbed forward trajectories. We formulate this as a min--max problem:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \max_{u\in\U(\rho)} \loss(\theta,u),
    \label{eq:minmax_generic}
\end{equation}

where the inner maximization selects a hard but feasible trajectory perturbation,
and the outer minimization learns a diffusion model that is robust to such perturbations. A score objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \loss(\theta,u)
    :=
    \frac12\int_0^T \lambda(t)
    \E_{X_t^u\sim p_t^u}
    \left[
    \norm{s_\theta(X_t^u,t)-\nabla_x\log p_t^u(X_t^u)}^2
    \right]dt,
    \label{eq:ideal_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda(t)\ge 0$ is a time weight. In practice, we do not optimize against the true score $\nabla_x \log p_t^u$, since it is unknown. Instead, we will introduce a standard diffusion training loss evaluated on perturbed noisy samples.

\section{Implementation}
For a practical experiment, we instantiate the robust objective above using the
standard denoiser training loss of a base diffusion model. We keep the reverse
sampler unchanged at test time, and use the controlled trajectory only as a
training-time mechanism for generating hard corrupted views.

\subsection{Generic practical objective}
Let $t \sim \pi$ be a sampling distribution over discrete corruption levels or
noise levels. For each clean sample $x_0 \sim \data$, we construct a coupled
reference path and controlled path up to the sampled corruption level $t$, and
we evaluate the base denoiser loss on the controlled noisy state. This gives
\begin{equation}
    \loss_{\mathrm{attack}}(\theta)
    :=
    \E_{x_0,\; t,\; \xi}
    \left[
        w(t)\,\ell_\theta(x_t^{\mathrm{ctrl}},x_0,t)
    \right],
    \label{eq:train_loss_attack}
\end{equation}
where $\xi$ denotes the randomness used in the shared-noise rollout, $w(t)\ge 0$
is a weighting over noise levels, and $\ell_\theta$ is the loss induced by the
chosen denoiser parameterization.

The clean branch uses the standard corruption process:
\begin{equation}
    \loss_{\mathrm{clean}}(\theta)
    :=
    \E_{x_0,\; t,\; \xi}
    \left[
        w(t)\,\ell_\theta(x_t^{\mathrm{ref}},x_0,t)
    \right].
    \label{eq:train_loss_clean}
\end{equation}

The practical min--max objective is then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
    \min_\theta \max_{\phi \in \Phi_{\kappa}}
    \;
    \loss_{\mathrm{clean}}(\theta)
    +
    \alpha\,\loss_{\mathrm{attack}}(\theta,\phi)
}
    \label{eq:main_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_{\kappa}$ denotes the class of control policies that satisfy the
hard per-step perturbation constraint defined below.

\subsection{Instantiation with EDM}
We use the EDM training parameterization of \cite{karras2022edm}. In EDM, the
corruption level is represented by a noise scale $\sigma$, so the attacked noisy
state is denoted by $x_\sigma^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$.

At the implementation level, we do not claim that $x_\sigma^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$
defines the exact marginal of a new generative forward law whose score is then
learned exactly. Instead, we treat $x_\sigma^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ as a robust
training view and apply the base denoiser loss to it:
\begin{equation}
    \ell_\theta^{\mathrm{EDM}}(x_\sigma,x_0,\sigma)
    :=
    \|D_\theta(x_\sigma,\sigma)-x_0\|^2.
    \label{eq:edm_loss_simple}
\end{equation}
Therefore the attacked branch becomes
\begin{equation}
    \loss_{\mathrm{attack}}^{\mathrm{EDM}}(\theta,\phi)
    :=
    \E_{x_0,\sigma,\xi}
    \left[
        w(\sigma)\,
        \|D_\theta(x_\sigma^{\mathrm{ctrl}},\sigma)-x_0\|^2
    \right],
    \label{eq:edm_attack_objective}
\end{equation}
with an analogous clean branch obtained by replacing
$x_\sigma^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ with $x_\sigma^{\mathrm{ref}}$.

This interpretation is deliberately modest: the proposal is a constrained robust
denoiser-training scheme over coupled forward paths, not a claim of exact score
matching for a generic controlled diffusion.

\section{Discrete-time formulation}
We use a discrete-time approximation as the primary implementation object. Let
\begin{equation}
    0=t_0<t_1<\dots<t_N=T,
    \qquad
    \sigma_0 < \sigma_1 < \dots < \sigma_N,
\end{equation}
and define the local diffusion increment
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \sigma_k := \sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_k.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Synchronous coupled rollout}
Given a clean sample $x_0$, we construct a reference path and a controlled path
using the same Gaussian innovations:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    x_{k+1}^{\mathrm{ref}}
    &=
    x_k^{\mathrm{ref}}
    +
    \Delta \sigma_k \,\varepsilon_k, \\
    x_{k+1}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}
    &=
    x_k^{\mathrm{ctrl}}
    +
    \Delta \sigma_k \,\varepsilon_k
    +
    \delta_k,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\varepsilon_k \sim \mathcal N(0,I).
\label{eq:discrete_coupled_rollout}
\end{equation}
The perturbation $\delta_k$ is produced by a control network
\begin{equation}
    \delta_k^{\mathrm{raw}}
    =
    g_\phi\!\left(
        x_k^{\mathrm{ref}},
        x_k^{\mathrm{ctrl}}-x_k^{\mathrm{ref}},
        \sigma_k
    \right),
    \label{eq:delta_raw}
\end{equation}
and then projected into a feasible local ball:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_k
    =
    \Pi_{\|\cdot\|\le \kappa \Delta \sigma_k}
    \left(
        \delta_k^{\mathrm{raw}}
    \right),
    \label{eq:delta_proj}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa>0$ is a dimensionless perturbation radius and
$\Pi_{\|\cdot\|\le r}$ denotes Euclidean projection onto the $\ell_2$ ball of
radius $r$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Equation \eqref{eq:delta_proj} constrains the controlled one-step kernel to
remain close to the reference one-step Gaussian kernel at every time step. This
acts as a local bicausal ambiguity set on the conditional innovations and
replaces the soft energy penalty used in the earlier draft.

\subsection{Discrete attacked objective}
Let $k$ denote a sampled training corruption level. Then the attacked denoiser
objective is
\begin{equation}
    \loss_{\mathrm{attack}}(\theta,\phi)
    =
    \E\left[
        w(k)\,
        \ell_\theta(x_k^{\mathrm{ctrl}},x_0,k)
    \right],
    \label{eq:discrete_attack_obj}
\end{equation}
while the clean branch is
\begin{equation}
    \loss_{\mathrm{clean}}(\theta)
    =
    \E\left[
        w(k)\,
        \ell_\theta(x_k^{\mathrm{ref}},x_0,k)
    \right].
    \label{eq:discrete_clean_obj}
\end{equation}
The resulting practical objective is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
    \min_\theta \max_{\phi}
    \;
    \loss_{\mathrm{clean}}(\theta)
    +
    \alpha\,\loss_{\mathrm{attack}}(\theta,\phi)
    \quad
    \text{s.t.}
    \quad
    \|\delta_k\| \le \kappa \Delta \sigma_k
    \ \text{for all } k.
}
    \label{eq:discrete_objective}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}
The role of $\kappa$ is fundamentally different from that of the previous
penalty coefficient $\lambda_1$. It specifies the radius of the feasible local
perturbation set directly, rather than tuning a soft regularization trade-off.
\end{remark}

\section{Minimal experimental plan}
The purpose of this draft is to support a simple implementation test. A minimal plan is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Toy 2D data.} Start with two moons or a Gaussian mixture, where support expansion can be visualized directly.
    \item \textbf{Base model.} Use a EDM denoiser.
    \item \textbf{Control network.} Use a shallow MLP $g_\phi(x^{\mathrm{ref}}_k, x^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_k-x^{\mathrm{ref}}_k, \sigma_k)$ that outputs a raw perturbation $\delta_k^{\mathrm{raw}}$, followed by an $\ell_2$ projection step enforcing $\|\delta_k\|\le \kappa \Delta\sigma_k$.
    \item \textbf{Objective.} Train with \eqref{eq:discrete_objective} using one or a few ascent steps for $\phi$ per descent step for $\theta$, together with a clean branch and a warmup/ramp schedule.
    \item \textbf{Baselines.} Compare against standard diffusion training and simple static data augmentation.
    \item \textbf{Readouts.} Evaluate sample diversity, coverage of low-density regions near the training manifold, and qualitative interpolation between modes.
\end{enumerate}

For images, then I will test can use a very low-data subset of CIFAR or another small dataset.

\section{Conclusion}
We have written a clean draft formulation for a robust diffusion training method under limited data.
% The central object is a perturbed forward SDE with a small adversarial control. The corresponding training problem is a min--max objective that replaces ordinary noisy training views by hard-but-plausible trajectory perturbations. The formulation is simple enough to implement on toy data, while still being formal enough to serve as a paper-style problem definition for discussion with advisors.
